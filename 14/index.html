The idea

  • One horizon. Everything built is a dark-timber box or cabinet exactly 2.4 m high. Above that line there is only concrete, glass and light, so the ceiling runs unbroken from wall to wall and the full 4 m is read everywhere. No joinery at 3.4 m that “almost” reaches: either clearly low, or nothing.
  • One long wall. The longest concrete wall is kept completely empty and washed with warm light from its foot to the ceiling. This is the room's one big gesture; nothing hangs on it.
  • One long line. Under that wall runs a single low bench, 40 cm high. The wall-wash light, a bio-ethanol fire line, the laser projector and its floor-rising 120″ screen, hi-fi and storage are all inside it. There is no TV: the screen rises for a film (toggle) and is gone again.
  • Emptiness on purpose. You arrive onto bare floor with the fire line and the lit wall ahead; the seating starts further in. The sofa is low and faces the wall; a backless leather daybed stands at the glass, so the window stays clear from floor to ceiling.
  • Soft against hard. 4 m sheer linen on a ceiling track covers the whole window wall when drawn, and is lit from the track at night: the black glass becomes a glowing veil. With the big wool rug it also takes the echo out of a concrete room, which is half of what “calm” means.
  • Mesh and wash don't mix, so they don't meet. The washed wall stays pure. The backlit steel mesh belongs to the study, where it is a tool wall; seen from the sofa through black steel glazing, the study glows like a lantern.
  • The island ends in a table. No dining table. The kitchen island's stone block carries on as a thick oak slab at the same height: two eat there daily, four face to face when needed, and all four stools live under the slab. It takes no floor of its own.
  • Cook by the glass, work behind a door. Here the kitchen and its table take the window corner, where guests end up and breakfast gets the sun; the kitchen's wall run hides behind the bathroom, so from the door you see only the island. The study takes the north-east corner: even north light on the screen, a window over the desk, a glass wall and a sliding door between it and the sofa. (A33 /13 tries it the other way round.)
  • The bedroom shrinks so the room can be whole. The bed is in a 2.9 × 2.5 m cabin, 4 m tall, with the window over it; clothes are in 2.3 m of wardrobes on the bathroom wall, right outside. In return no room touches the long east wall, and the door-to-glass axis is free.

Check before building

  • Bio-ethanol burner: needs no flue, but check the housing company's rules and keep the specified clearances; an automatic burner with sensors is the safe kind. It is 2 m or more from the curtains.
  • Projection: an ultra-short-throw laser projector in the bench with a motorised floor-rising ALR screen (120″, 2.66 × 1.5 m). The bench needs ventilation and power; the wash is switched off when the screen is up.
  • The wall wash is a continuous LED grazer in the bench's rear edge, about 5 cm off the wall. Grazing light shows every flaw in the concrete: look at the wall with a torch held flat against it before committing.
  • Glass above the 2.4 m boxes is fixed and frameless. It keeps sound in but lets light through; the bedroom needs a blind on the track inside if you sleep while lights are on outside.
  • The steel-and-glass screen is 4 m high: fixed to floor and ceiling, laminated glass, sliding door hung from the 2.4 m transom.
  • Sink and dishwasher stay on the wall by the shaft; the island has only the induction hob (recirculating downdraft, no hood), so it needs power in the floor but no drain.
  • No ceiling lights in the living room at all: wall wash, veil, fire, the study lantern and one reading lamp. Dimmable in scenes.
  • Dimensions come from the 1:100 sales plan, good to about 10 cm.
  • The sleeping cabin is small on purpose: a 180 bed on a wall-to-wall platform, a 60 cm ledge beside it, 70 cm to step in. If that is too tight, the cabin can grow 40 cm east at the cost of the entry's width (1.7 m now).
  • The kitchen uses the southern kitchen reservation; confirm the drain and water points with EKE.
